Process Investigation Workspace · Design note

Reactor-02 · Batch 4471 · incident 15 Sep 2026

Three most important decisions

  1. The trend is the screen. Signals are stacked in lanes on one shared time axis. Each lane keeps its own engineering units, and its scale always includes the alarm limits. Lanes are ordered by first deviation, and numbered markers (1–5) sit where each signal left normal. Alarms are drawn on the signal that raised them, not in a separate list. Engineers read cause and effect from the order of change, so the layout shows that order directly.
  2. Grey until abnormal. Following ISA-101, traces are neutral grey. Only the segment beyond a limit turns amber (H / LO) or red (HH), using ISA-18.2 priority colours. Every colour also has a shape (▲ / ◆) and a text label, and the series colours are checked for colour-blind safety. A light-grey day theme and a dark night theme share the same alarm meaning and switch at shift handover, for long sessions and night shifts.
  3. Answer first, evidence you can check. The side panel opens with the recommended first action. It gives the cause in one line and confidence as "3 of 4 checks agree". Each check is a fact the engineer can verify on the trend, and the one open question (valve or pump?) is stated plainly. No black-box score, and the supervisor gets a call before 10:00.

What I chose not to show

  • Health score (62/100). It restates severity without changing the decision. I kept predicted trip risk (78% within 4 h) because it drives urgency.
  • Raw alarm log. The three Info rows duplicate the event log, and the others are already plotted on their signals.
  • Feed flow and agitator current lanes. Both are normal all day, so they collapse into one "2 signals normal" row that the engineer can expand.
  • Asset tree, inspection date, lab sample details. These are useful for orientation once, not for this decision. The header carries what matters.
  • Multiple y-axes and an overview strip. They add reading effort without adding insight.

Assumptions

  • "Normal" is each signal's average over the first 12 h. Onset is where the first signal starts a sustained move away from it: coolant flow falls from 08:50 and is beyond 3σ by 08:54, 25 min before the temperature alarm.
  • A backup cooling route exists and the supervisor can authorise it. The process engineer recommends, and operations acts.
  • Temperature A is the primary sensor and B is redundant. B's 09:08–09:13 gap is a data-quality dropout.
  • Health and trip risk come from the product's analytics and are taken as given. Screen C's 25 extra signals are illustrative.

What I'd test with engineers first

  • Do they agree the incident started at 08:50, and would they trust a system-estimated onset?
  • Is "% of the way to limit" read correctly within seconds, compared with the normalised overlays they use today?
  • Does "3 of 4 checks agree" build more trust than a percentage confidence?
  • Are they comfortable with normal signals collapsed by default, or do they want everything visible?

A weakness in an existing tool: PI Vision

PI Vision users report on AVEVA's own feedback portal that multi-scale trends hide when a value left its operating window, because each trace auto-scales to its data and not to its limits. Here, every lane's scale always includes its H / HH / LO limits, the exceedance is drawn in alarm colour, and comparing different units uses one shared % of the way to alarm limit axis instead of stacked scales.

ADefault · last 24 h

  • The full 24 h stays, as asked. The flat first 23 h is evidence too: no slow drift overnight.
  • The excursion fills only the last few percent of the axis, so it's marked with an incident band from 08:50 and one "Zoom to incident" action.
  • The header answers severity, start time, affected asset, trip risk and decision deadline at a glance.
  • The CV-204 maintenance window is hatched, so the 08:15–08:45 coolant dip reads as planned, not as a fault.

BZoomed & compared

  • 09:00–09:25 is where every limit crossing happens. The cursor is parked at 09:15, the first temperature alarm.
  • Compare plots each signal from its baseline (0%) to its alarm limit (100%). The crossings read as a lead–lag chain: flow 09:03 → return temp 09:10 → reactor temp 09:15 → pressure 09:24.
  • Own-unit lanes stay underneath on the same cursor, so real values are never lost.
  • Feed flow is drawn dotted near 0%, which shows visually why it's ruled out.

CStress case · 33 signals

  • Sorted by first deviation. Only the 11 deviating signals get lanes, and the 22 normal ones collapse into asset groups with counts.
  • Lanes go compact (52 px) but keep value, unit and alarm state, so they're readable without hovering.
  • The panel says what the new signals changed: CV-204 position diverges from controller output, so confidence moves to 4 of 4 and pump P-204 is ruled out.
  • The added signals are labelled as illustrative.
